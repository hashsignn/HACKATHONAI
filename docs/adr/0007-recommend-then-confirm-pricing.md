# ADR 7: The platform recommends tiers; a named manager confirms them

**Status:** accepted

## Context

The jury asked who decides the price and whether it could be biased. A fully automatic price hides judgment inside a model; a fully manual price invites inconsistency and favouritism.

## Decision

From the intake, published rules **recommend** a complexity tier (points for value at stake, duration, cross-border, bespoke terms) and a consequence tier (who relies on the deliverable, raised to the service's minimum), with the reasons. A **named manager or partner confirms** them. Any override needs a written reason. Confirmation runs the guardrails, fixes the review level and signers, and **locks the quote** into the ledger. Client importance is not an input to price; it can only raise review intensity, at KPMG's cost.

## Consequences

- Every price has a name and a reason attached, like every other judgment on the platform.
- Quality & Risk sees confirmations and overrides per manager in the reviewer record, which is the check on bias.
- Invoices never change when the price list does; a new version applies to new engagements only.
